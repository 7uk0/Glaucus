Salvaging is a very lucrative way to spend your time on the Frontier, and is one of the largest ways that spesos are added to the overall economy. Salvaging is very consistent, reasonably safe, and can be done with basically zero investment, and so is a pretty good way for new Frontierspeople to make enough money to start taking on bigger things.

As a note, this is intended as a basic salvaging guide. Generally salvaging is done in tandem with mining, which is not covered here. Additionally, salvaging off of derelicts or other non-wreck locations is not covered. This is just to get your started, and give you context for what people might be asking for or talking about in-game.

Ship Choice[edit]

There's no way to go wrong with which ship you pick, as basic salvaging takes very little investment. The current slate of salvaging ships are all great for what you need.

The specific shuttles available to use change on a pretty regular basis. To find a shuttle that's good for easy salvage, choose "Small" and "Salvage" from the dropdowns in the Shipyard Menu. Any of the shuttles available there will be appropriate for this purpose. In general, you want to make sure you're spending less than half your money on a ship.

At time of writing the Surveyor is a great place to start. It has all the machines you need to get going, both for salvaging and mining. Everything it doesn't come stock with, you can make without any further research.

If you have a little more cash to burn, the Chisel is a great upgrade. It has a bunch of extra machines that you won't need to build yourself, and it comes stock with a dumpster and construction box, which make salvaging (and mining) much more convenient. Those are described in detail in the Powering Up section.

Once you buy a ship, look around for your salvage bay. This is a set of shutters than can be opened with a button, and allow you to enter and leave your ship without waiting for an airlock to open. Keep in mind where the salvage bay is on your ship for later.

What To Buy[edit]

You'll need a few things from the Frontier Outpost before you head out salvaging.

Medical Program - Medbay vendor - An item that you can add to your PDA that turns it into an infinite-use Health Analyzer. Very helpful when you've been hit by damage you don't recognize and want to know how to heal yourself. You can start with this in your loadout, but it's cheaper from the vendor.

Medical Implant - Medbay vendor or your Loadout - An item that you can use on yourself to implant yourself with a medical implant. This implant will alert medics to your location when you eventually get bitten to death by space carp. This is a default item in your loadout, so you only need to buy it separately if you change your loadout to remove it. You also want to make sure to turn your suit sensors on: right click your clothes, click Sensors, then Coordinates.

Bruise Packs - Medbay vendor - At least one pack is very useful. You'll get attacked by animals sometimes, and while there are ways to heal without consumables, these are cheap enough to be well worth it.

Blood Packs - Medbay vendor - Same deal. Blood loss isn't generally a huge issue when salvaging, but blood packs are cheap enough that having an extra won't hurt.

Medical HUD - Medbay vendor - This is much less necessary, but it'll let you know at a glance both what your own health is and what your enemies health is when fighting, which is helpful when you're getting the hang of combat.

Emergency Double Oxygen Canister - Air can vendor - This is the yellow can in the air can vendor, specifically. This is a small canister that fits in your pockets rather than your suit slot, and has enough oxygen that you won’t need to worry overmuch about running out on wrecks. Just be sure to fill up back on the ship before you go out again. Having your suit slot open is helpful for Jetpacks, covered below.

What Not To Buy[edit]

There are several useful items that you really don't need to buy before heading out salvaging.

Gauze - You will find a ton of cloth, and will almost certainly find enough to make the 10 gauze you can buy from the machine on your first wreck. Just take the cloth in your hand, press G to open the crafting menu, search “gauze”, and craft a bunch of them. You'll want to keep this around with your other medical supplies. Use one after each fight where you bleed to reduce ongoing damage.

EVA suit - Each of these ships has lockers with salvaging suits. Suit lockers are embedded in walls on the Small Salvage ships, in lockers marked "EVA".

A Jetpack - Likewise, this is already on your ship. Make absolute certain you have a jetpack on you at all times until you get used to moving around the edges of wrecks safely. It’s very easy to send yourself flinging into space, and it’s hard to save yourself unless you already know what to do. With a jetpack, just put it on, turn it on, and you can fly wherever you want. Put this in your suit slot, the one on the far right. This can also go on your belt, but you’ll be using your belt for Construction Bags, and having a jetpack is a big deal when you’re just learning.

Fuel - Both Uranium and Plasma are plentiful on wrecks, and you'll find several stacks of both on practically every wreck. Fuel from the vendor isn't very expensive, but it's just not needed.

Tools - Fully-stocked Utility Belts are on most of these ships, all tools can be made with the Salvage Techfab that's on all of them, and you'll often find full toolbelts on wrecks.

A Gun - Weaponry in general isn't usually useful when salvaging. Most of the Small Salvage ships have a stock PKA, which is plenty of firepower for your needs. The ones that don't have one stock can print on with the Salvage Techfab.

Matter Reclaimer flatpack - Trash is a great way to get a lot of different resources, and you're definitely going to want one of these. However, you can print a board for it and make your own very cheaply, rather than spending a few thousand spesos on a whole flatpack.

Heading Out[edit]

After undocking from the FO and flying out, you want to go to at least 5km from FO and look for blue jagged balls on your mass scanner. There are multiple shades of blue, so if you pull up to one and it's a rock/ice asteroid, that's the wrong color. Keep going until you find one that looks like a broken-open space platform.

(Author’s Note: I have poor color vision and have a hell of a time telling the different blue objects apart. I don't really have advice on how to tell which is which, I just guess and hope and move on if I get it wrong. Thankfully, ice asteroids have been moved out to the 9-15km ring, so if you're between 5 and 9 km from FO then the only blue shapes you're likely to see are wrecks. Check Sector Topology in your guidebook for more details.)

Once you're there, place your salvage bay near one side of it and get ready to hop over. Make sure there’s at least a few tiles of space in between your bay and the wreck, so enemies can’t hop directly into your ship.

On your first wreck, you won’t have the supplies to make everything you need, but the overall strategy is the same.

  • Grab your PKA and empty your backpack.
  • Hop onto the wreck, and open every box you see. You're looking for cloth and steel in particular on your first run, and also anything else that you can stuff in your backpack.
    • Also look for any extra tools you might find useful. A t-ray scanner, industrial/experimental welding tools, a spare Oxygen canister if you have space, and a nicer EVA suit if you'd like are all useful.
  • If you see animals moving around, go to Harm mode and click your PKA to wield it in two hands. Keep as much distance as you can and shoot the carp/spider/bear/etc.
    • Most enemies are simple animals who don't have a ranged attack. However, they can still chase you out into space. Be sure to keep moving and always be aware of where the enemy you're fighting is, and where any other nearby enemies are. If possible, pull one enemy off the grid towards you at a time, and try to avoid pulling multiple at once.
    • If you get bit by a spider, get back to your ship immediately. They have a poison that makes you fall asleep eventually. On your ship it's annoying, when you're out on a wreck with hostiles around, it's deadly. If you can’t get back to your ship, lock yourself inside a box or locker on the wreck and call out on the radio for help. Be sure your suit coordinates are on (right click your basic clothes -> Sensor -> Coordinates). Click the red hat button on your action bar to wake back up.
    • If you see a creature with flapping wings, be very, very careful. That's a Wyvern, and it can both fly off the wreck, and has a projectile breath attack. Keep your jetpack active, keep moving, and concentrate more on not getting hit than on hitting it back, and you'll be okay.
    • If you accidentally float off the wreck and run out of jetpack gas, aim the PKA away from the wreck/your ship and fire it a bunch of times. When you’re in space, the PKA has enough kick to send you backwards, and it can be used as a very slow jetpack. If you don't have that, make sure your suit sensors are on (right click your basic clothes -> Sensor -> Coordinates) and say so on the radio. People will often come help if you're stranded.
  • If you want to go fast, ignore anything else that isn't a construction material. You want steel, plasma, Uranium, glass, cloth, plasteel, gold, silver, stuff like that. Ignore jewelry and coins, ignore barrels.
    • If you want to spend a little extra time, you can grab some of the other valuables. Rings with gems, gold masks, lamps, gold watches, and the top two values of coin are the most worth it. The most valuable coins are blue, the second are grey with two side-by-side diamond shapes. Quickly grab these and press Shift-B to put them into your backpack. Once on your ship, you can move coins to your wallet for easy storage.

When you get back, dump all the construction materials into the salvage tech fab. If you examine it, you'll see a line that says something about a magnet. Turn it on if it isn't already, the toggle is in the right-click menu. As soon as you drop a valid item on the fab, it'll get sucked in. This makes it much easier to handle large piles of items. Additionally, you can make a very important item from this fab once you have steel and cloth:

Construction Bags - You need at least two. Put one on your belt and carry one in your hand. Make sure both have the Magnet turned on and a bag on your belt slot. As soon as you walk near any construction material, it'll get sucked into the bags. Once you're back on the ship, go to the techfab, place the bags on the techfab with Q, right click each one, and click Dump Out on Ground. All the mats will land on the fab and get sucked in by the magnet. You can dump out both at once, just put them both down and right click->dump out both in turn.

Once you've got your bags, you can stop using your backpack for storage. Head onto a wreck, open all the boxes, grab all the loose construction mats, dump them in the fab, move on. Once you get into a rhythm doing a full wreck like this only takes a few minutes. You'll likely need a few trips to get all the materials from a wreck.

The one exception to the fab sucking in all mats is uranium. You'll end up with a pile of uranium on top of it, so be careful about walking nearby with your construction bag magnets on. If your ship uses uranium for fuel, you can put the uranium you find in the fuel locker next to your generator for safekeeping.

Repeat this process as long as you'd like, accumulating more resources from each wreck. Be sure to occasionally pull out Plasma/Uranium to fuel your generator, and be sure to turn on your generator’s magnet as well so you don't need to keep manually filling it.

Collecting Trash[edit]

Something that is very helpful to do but isn't strictly necessary is collecting all the trash you find on wrecks and putting it in your Material Reclaimer. Trash on wrecks is worth a lot of resources, and collecting it is a great way to make extra money.

The easy way to do this is to use a dumpster, described below. If you don't have the extra 1000 spesos to spare, then look for anything on the wreck called "broken" or that looks like it's a torn-off or damaged thing. Take everything you can find and dump it into one of the crates on the wreck.

Bring the crate to your Matter Reclaimer (ctrl-click it to drag, q to release), and drop everything inside onto the reclaimer. It'll take a minute to process, but eventually anything you put onto it will break down into its component parts. Walk next to it with an active Construction Bag and everything will get sucked up and ready to be dumped on the techfab.

Sometimes you'll see larger broken machines on wrecks, like broken generators or solar panels. You use these in much the same way: Drag them, bring them to the matter reclaimer, and manually move them to be touching the reclaimer (ctrl-right click to move something you're dragging). They'll be destroyed like any other object, and the reclaimer will turn them to a large number of various resources.

If you don't already have a matter reclaimer aboard your ship, you can make one in the Salvage Techfab. Print the board, and get at least 10 steel, 1 piece of lv cable, 5 plastic, and 2 Manipulators (can be printed from the salvage techfab).

The process for making any machine is the same.

  • First, with at least 5 steel in hand, press G to open the construction menu. Search for "Machine Frame". Click Place Construction Ghost, then click on the spot you want to place the machine. Right click to exit ghost placement mode.
  • Click the ghost with the steel.
  • Use a Wrench to secure the machine frame.
  • Add 1 lv cable, then the machine board. Then shift-click to examine the machine and it will tell you what else you need to add.
  • Take each of those ingredients in hand and click the machine with them.
  • Once you've added everything you need, it will say "construction complete" on the machine.
  • Use a Screwdriver to finish, and it will become the machine you want to make.

On small ships like these, you can place a machine pretty much anywhere and it will be powered. If it isn't powered right away, use a Crowbar to pry up tiles until you find green lv cable. Make more lv cables and place them in your underfloor to get closer to the new machine. Once the cable is within a few squares, the machine should turn on.

Be warned though, you might need to increase the power output on your generator if you add more than one or two. Click your generator and check the "Network" text on the bottom of it. The left-hand number is your output, the right hand number is your consumption. If you consume more than you make, change the power output of your generator to be a little above your consumption. You can also unwrench any machines you're not actively using, and they'll stop drawing power.

Selling[edit]

When you're tired of salvaging, head to the Trade Mall. The Trade Mall gives much more money for sold goods than the Cargo Depots or other places to sell, and it's very worth it to make the trip. To double check before selling that you're in the right place, you can examine the Cargo Sale Computer and it will tell you the level of profit you make compared to Market Rate. You want to sell at a place with 1.25x profit.

If it's not on your main Nav menu when piloting your ship, go to the middle Map tab, select Search for Objects, wait for the menu to populate, and find the Trade Mall there. Click the target button to set your selected target as the Trade Mall, and fly over there.

Once you find the Trade Mall, dock in one of the CB (cargo bay) docking ports. Use a wrench to unanchor your techfab and drag it over to the selling area. Bring over the uranium and anything else you've salvaged, and drop that all on the sell pad as well. Once everything you’ve salvaged is on the selling area grates, go to the selling computer nearby and sell it all. This will sell your techfab as well, getting you back some of the money for it, but if you’re done for the day you don’t need it anymore anyway.

(Author’s Note: You used to have to remove all of the materials from the techfab in order to sell them. This isn’t the case anymore, you can sell the fab and all of its components for the same price you would get taking them out first. If someone tells you a trick to crack open a fab to get all the materials to drop out, this is what they’re talking about, and that trick isn’t needed anymore.)

Once you're done with all that, find a Shipyard Console on the Trade Mall (there's one in the area marked ATMS on the map), sell your ship, then head to the Cryo room on the station to cryo. Always make sure to sell your ship before you cryo, both to remove an extra ship from the sector, and to make back most of the money you spent on it. Nothing but your bank account stay between rounds. And it's always better to cryo than to just log out, so your unmoving body isn't clogging up the halls.

And that's basically it! Do these things and you'll make a reliable profit from salvaging.

Powering Up[edit]

There are a number of things you can get that make your salvage experience better.

Friends - Bringing on crew makes salvaging both much faster, and also more social. Making friends makes the Frontier less lonely. The computer next to the shuttle computer will allow you to open crew slots, so when someone is logging into the game they can see your ship and decide to spawn there instead of at the FO. Make sure you have enough suits for everyone before you add a slot, though.

Magboots - With these, you no longer float when on wrecks. Gives much more control over your movement both on wrecks and when nearby your ship. Can be found often on wrecks, or in certain EVA suit lockers. Turn them off when you’re not on a wreck, they make you walk slower whenever they’re on.

Boxes and Dumpsters - Some salvage ships have these stock, everyone else needs to go to the Trade Mall cargo bays to buy them. On each cargo bay, there's two computers across from the sell pad computer. You're looking for the Cargo Request computer, which has a list of various items to buy. The two that are useful here are the Construction Box and Dumpster. The Construction Box works just like the Construction Bag, but is much larger. You drag it behind you rather than putting it on your belt. Alt-click it to turn the magnet on an off. One box will clear out all the basic resources on a wreck in a single trip. The Dumpster is the same thing, but for trash items. Turn the magnet on, drag it across the wreck, and it'll grab all the junk that you'd normally put in a crate. These make salvage a lot easier, and they're only 1000 spesos each, so unless it's your very first time out and cash is extremely tight, they're almost always worth it.

Material Conveyors - This is a common upgrade made to a lot of ships to make moving materials from one machine to another easier.

  • First, turn off the power to your Material Reclaimer, then unwrench the reclaimer and techfab and move them so they're next to each other, then wrench them back down. Leave the Reclaimer off until you're done, so you don't accidentally destroy a tool.
  • In the Salvage Techfab, make two Conveyor Belts, and two lv cable. You'll also need 4 Steel.
  • In the G menu, search Conveyor Belt, and place one facing your Material Reclaimer, and one under the reclaimer facing the Techfab.
  • With the conveyor belts you've printed in hand, click the conveyor ghosts. You can also right click the tile the ghost is on and then left click the ghost's entry to build it that way, if something is in the way.
  • In the G menu, build two Two-Way Levers. They need 2 steel and 1 lv cable each.
  • Get a multitool. Click one lever, then one of the belts. Connect the Left and Right positions to Forward, then Middle to Off. Press E on the lever to turn it on and make sure it's going in the right direction. If it's going the wrong way, click the lever and belt again, Clear your settings, and set Left and Right to Reverse instead. Do the same on the second belt. You'll likely have to right click the tile to bring up the conveyor belt then left click the entry in the list to connect to it.

Now, you can bring all of your boxes onto the first conveyor, and dump them out on the ground there. Turn on the belt, and it'll all be moved onto the Material Reclaimer. Then, once everything is converted to its material parts, you turn on the second belt to move it all onto the Salvage Techfab. Two levers, and everything gets moved automatically.

Medical Bed - In the Construction menu (press G) you can find the Medical Bed, which requires some durathread and plasteel to make. With plastic and cloth you can make your own durathread. These are very useful for getting a little bit of healing in between wrecks. In just a minute or two you can go from significantly hurt to just fine, though it doesn’t heal all damage types.

Scrap Processor - You get a lot more scrap from mining scrap asteroids, but you get at least some from throwing stuff into the Material Reclaimer. You can print a board for this in the Salvage Techfab and build one yourself. It takes two kinds of scrap, you can check the crafting menu in the processor to see what they're called and look like. The regular scrap turns into random resources, including some very valuable ones like Bluespace Crystals. The tech scrap turns into random machine boards and blueprints.

Construction Bag of Holding - Generally requires doing a bit of mining to get the bluespace crystals needed, and also requires finding a science ship with the requisite research, but if you can mine or buy the materials and pay a Science ship to make it, this will hold much, much more of the construction materials without needing to go back to the ship and dump them all out. A huge upgrade, if you can get it.

Experimental Salvage Hardsuit - Has a good flashlight and great defense. Generally not needed for basic salvage, but nice if you can get one. Much more useful for mining, or for derelicts or other places with more dangerous enemies. Can be printed by Science.
