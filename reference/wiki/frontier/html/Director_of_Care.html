The Director of Care is responsible for keeping the sector healthy. A decent DoC makes sure emergency response medics get paid, a great one makes sure no one stays dead long enough to rot.

This guide is mostly intended for people who either are already whitelisted, or are actively pursuing a whitelist. It assumes that you have interacted with a DoC before and generally know how the game works, and just need help with the details.

DoC Basics[edit]

The DoC has a few specific tools available to them that need explaining.

Medical Dispatch[edit]

The station itself is one of your most important tools. While it's built more as a waystation than a hospital, it's very common for people to come there for medical help. The bar is a common point for people to gather at the end of their shifts. As the only place to buy medical ships, you'll get a lot of people coming through to pick up a ship for medical hybrid work.

Most importantly, there's an empty room right next to the DoC's office for you to do whatever you want with. Many DoCs use this room as a cryomed lab, others use it as a clinic, a botany bay, really anything you'd like to use it for. There are supplies provided to you to build this out; there's a Command-locked room behind the Janitor's closet at one end of the station where you can find a cryotube and freezer flatpack, as well as an empty chem dispenser and a several crates full of various resources. You have the authority to use these as you see fit, which often means making a nice little hospital room to do cryo bounties in.

In general, you have the authority to make any changes you'd like to MD, as long as the changes you make don't disrupt the basic functions of the station. Be cautious before making an addition that might take away business from other roles, like a functioning kitchen or publicly available research server.

The DoC's Office[edit]

The DoC has a number of consoles in their office that help them do the various functions of their job.

Station Administration and Announcement Console[edit]

The Station Administration console is your station bank account. You are generally allowed to use it as you see fit, as long as you do so reasonably. The definition of "reasonable" is intentionally vague, but part of getting whitelisted is the admins and community in general trusting your judgement and roleplay sense. So trust yourself; if you think an expense is reasonable, it probably is.

To withdraw, put an amount to withdraw, pick the reason closest to what you're withdrawing it for, give a short description of what you're getting it for, and click Withdraw. The admins will check these sometimes, but as long as you're doing something reasonable there won't be an issue. Taking 10,000 spesos for "Buying food" is fine, taking 1,000,000 spesos for "funny clown show" probably isn't.

To deposit, insert spesos just like you would for an ATM, and fill out the deposit fields just like you would the withdrawal fields.

The Announcement Console is used to send messages to the whole sector. Generally, you just enter your announcement in the field and click the "announce" button. The drop-down menu with colors is there to set the sector alert level, you should not be editing that unless instructed to by the SR or Sheriff.

You also have a shuttle console for the MD itself. This works like a normal shuttle console, where you can undock ships, scan the sector, and keep an eye out for ship-based trouble. If you're coordinating medical response, you can use this to find out how far away different ships are to the alert.

By default, you will spawn in with flatpacks for the Station Administration console and Announcement Console. These are also found in the DoC's office, but the ones you spawn in with can be brought aboard a ship that you buy and used to access these functions while away from MD. It's relatively common for DoCs to purchase a large ship to use as a mobile hospital, and these two consoles cover what you might need to not use MD at all.

Your PDA[edit]

Your PDA has two functions not available to normal contractors.

The first is the Spesoflow Lite app. This app lets you track your budget, showing you where money is coming in and being spent. Your budget will mostly come from Vaults, Taxes, and Medical Bounties.

The second is Command Bounties. In the normal Bounty app, there's a second Command tab that is only available to Command personnel. You can use this to put up bounties for anything you need, and given the smaller number of people posting there, it'll remain more visible through the round. Remember to take down any bounties you put up before you cryo, though.

DoC's Desk[edit]

It might sound weird to call out something that has little functional use, but the DoC's desk is great for interviews, medical debriefs, or just generally creating a professional atmosphere when you do business. If you're having people sign documents in person, or turn in slips, doing so at the desk, with two chairs facing each other, can heighten the immersion of the RP.

Uniforms[edit]

In the small room you spawn in, there's a locker containing a medical director's hardsuit, as well as a number of pieces of clothing that are specific to the DoC. If you want to be easily recognized, wearing a distinctive hat and mantle is a great way to do that. Just be sure to bring it back before you cryo so the next DoC can use them if they want.

DoC Duties[edit]

The Director of Care has much less strongly-defined duties than the SR or Sheriff. You are free to pursue the goal of making the sector healthier however you see fit. As a whitelisted player, you have the trust of the community and admin team to be able to run a department in a way that enhances the overall RP experience of the game, and this trust means that there are very few hard rules outside of the basic "pay people" and "don't do crime".

That said, there are a few common threads in how people play Director of Care.

Authority[edit]

In short, your authority extends as far as the contracts you make. The only formal authority the DoC has is the authority to withdraw money from the station budget, and the authority to modify MD. The primary way you have to affect the sector is by hiring people to do medical work on your behalf, and issuing commands as allowed by that contractual relationship.

What this means is that your medical department is much more strongly influenced by your priorities than other departments might be. The SR and Sheriff have defaults for how many roles of each type can be played, and while some will adjust these numbers, it's much more rare for SRs and Sheriffs to actively recruit for their ranks in-round. Whereas no one spawns in as one of the DoC's employees, and everyone must be recruited manually.

Staff[edit]

It's common for DoCs to seek out and hire a few emergency responders. Typically, these people will be paid 15-20k/hr, with 10k as the minimum wage, like any paid role. Some DoCs will ask that responders collect signed receipts when they save someone as proof of work. Some DoCs will also give a bounty (typically in the 1-2k range) for each turned-in receipt. Some DoCs believe this encourages infighting among medics and don't offer a defined bounty. Even when there is no bounty, most DoCs appreciate being given signed healing receipts as proof that work was done. The precise amount given will depend on the medical budget that shift. Remember that it's better to offer a low salary and give a larger bonus than the other way around; if you're flush with cash it's easy to spread, but if you're broke then you might not have the cash to pay everyone.

When you pay someone, it's generally good practice to round up to the nearest hour and note exactly which hours they're being paid for. So if someone is hired at 2:00 and cryos at 4:40, pay them three hours pay, up through 5:00. It's usually a good idea to pay a contractor what you owe them whenever they end up on station, just so you can pay in smaller chunks instead of one huge paycheck at the end. This will give you a better sense of what your budget actually is. Sometimes you might have to fire someone if they're doing an exceptionally poor job, but even then you should always pay someone for the hours that they actually did work.

DoCs can also hire cryomed staff. One of the best ways for the MD to make money is turning in cryo bounties. Any time a bounty is turned in, 75% of the listed value of the bounty is added to the MD station account. When the DoC turns in the bounty, the listed value is also added to the station account, instead of being handed to the DoC as cash. So whatever value you see for a medical bounty, add 75% for the total value of the bounty to the MD once turned in.

A typical cryomed crew will be one chemist, one botanist, and one or two salvagers. The salvager(s) go out to find the medical pods. The botanist will grow necessary plants (aloe and stellibinin are pretty vital), and the chemist will mix the chems and put them in the patient. Sometimes the DoC can take one or more of these roles, but a steady supply of bounty pods can more than pay for the cost of 3-4 employees.

If you're doing cryomed bounties, then you might need to buy more frozen bodies from people. It's relatively common to have an open bounty for medical bounties, so salvagers who find them can get a better payout than they would selling them to Trade, and you can heal them and cash them in for a profit. Around 5000 spesos is a typical bounty price (at the time of writing), which means that you'll likely make about 5000-10000 spesos from each healed body. You can put up these bounties in the normal bounty app, call them out on Common, or even holocall salvage teams directly.

Sometimes a DoC will purchase a large medical ship and operate out of there, using the station admin console and announcement console you spawn in with to access those functions while away from MD. Flying on a ship makes it much easier to find your own pods to heal, and gives you a lot more in the way of resources to heal people on the fly. On the other hand, it makes you a bit harder to find; Generally people expect to find the DoC at MD, and can get confused when you're not there.

The Law[edit]

One important thing to note about your duties is that enforcing the law in general is NOT one of your duties. Enforcing docking law specifically on MD is something you can do, though you'll need to call for a Guard or NFSD to come help if anyone is belligerent. As a member of command, you are never allowed to use C3 contraband, cannot encourage anyone to pick up or use C3 contraband, and cannot accept it as a gift except to immediately impound it for the NFSD to pick up later. However, you are not required to actually enforce the law, including contraband possession law. You do not have the authority to perform an arrest of any kind. If someone is breaking the law in your presence, you can ask them to stop or call the NFSD to come help, but you are not empowered to detain, search, or arrest even the most blatant of criminals.

In particular, you should familiarize yourself with the Space Law around contraband chemicals. the relevant law can be found here. In general, chemicals that are mind-affecting are C2, and ones that mostly do damage or are otherwise very dangerous are C3. Be on the lookout for C3 chems, it's pretty common to be given large batches of random chems, and you shouldn't be keeping anything that's C3.

Different DoCs have different policies for handling law breaks. Some will call the NFSD every time with as much information as they can. Some will generally give a heads up that contraband is floating around. Some will ignore breaches of the law that don't directly affect the overall health of the people in the sector. Remember that you're roleplaying a member of sector command, and that you should generally cooperate with the NFSD unless you have good in-round reason not to. But you aren't required to know any Space Law outside the parts that reference the DoC directly, and can be pretty hands-off with other people breaking the law as long as you're not actively encouraging them.

Paying Other Departments[edit]

Finally, as a member of command, you are encouraged by server rules to help pay FO/NFSD/PP staff if there is no corresponding command member to pay already-hired staff members. You generally can't hire new staff (and you absolutely shouldn't be hiring Cadets or other NFSD personnel) but if someone has taken a default role or was hired by a since-cryo'd Command member, you should pay them from the appropriate station budget if you have the time to do so.

Paperwork[edit]

You spawn in with a Medical Doctor's Folder, and you can find more of these in the DoC's office. This has a number of important pieces of paperwork, you should familiarize with each of them. When you need to use one, put the blank copy in the fax machine and click Copy to create a fresh blank copy, then put the original back in the folder. This way, you always have a blank to use and never need to find a new folder.

There are a few pieces of paperwork that don't come stock in the MD's folder which might be useful to you.

First, the Medical Responder contract. This contract allows you to hire someone to do medical response, going out to rescue the dead or dying and bring them back to fighting trim. This also includes license to make/store/sell C1/C2 chems, though you should feel free to remove that section if you don't want to give that license to all responders.

Do Not Resuscitate[edit]

Made by Discord user Ducktapeal.

The DNR is one of the most extreme things a DoC can do. If someone shows a clear pattern of absolutely disrespecting the time and RP of your medical staff, you can issue a notice that your staff is not to continue wasting time and resources on them. This does not make it a crime to heal someone under a DNR order, it just means that you, as DoC, won't pay for it. The worst consequence you can deliver for someone violating a DNR is terminating their medical contract.

Only issue a DNR if you are ABSOLUTELY SURE that someone is a waste of medical time. Examples include repeated and clearly intentional suicide or repeatedly assaulting medical crew. A DNR is pretty close to a round removal, which is typically reserved for capital crimes. If you feel like someone is deserving of a DNR, you should almost certainly also be Ahelping the situation, as the player is being disrespectful of the MRP environment we want to encourage here.

DNRs are extremely rare, and the average DoC won't give out more than one or two in their whole career. But if you do need to send one, here is a paperwork template to use:
