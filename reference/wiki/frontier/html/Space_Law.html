Jurisdiction[edit]

All vessels inside NanoTrasen space, as defined by the entirety of the Frontier Sectors or registered under NanoTrasen operate under abbreviated Space Law. All crew, passengers, and visitors aboard a NanoTrasen vessel, within NanoTrasen space or in the employ of NanoTrasen are beholden to this law and expected to follow it, and is enforced within the sector by the New Frontier Sheriff's Department.

Common sense knowledge should be sufficient to not break the law. The details are of particular importance to the New Frontier Sheriff's Department and Station Administration.

One should note that exterior systems, such as the legal structures and systems of other jurisdictions, do not have bearing in this jurisdiction and should not be used as a direct reference.

Basic Rights[edit]

Under Space Law, all NanoTrasen contractors and employees are granted the following basic rights:

  • NanoTrasen shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof; or abridging the freedom of speech, or of the press; or the right of the employees peaceably to assemble, and to petition NanoTrasen for a redress of grievances.
  • The right of the employees to be secure in their persons, vessels, papers, and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be violated, and no warrants shall issue but upon reasonable cause, supported by oath or affirmation, and particularly describing the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.
  • Excessive bail shall not be required, nor excessive fines imposed, nor cruel and unusual punishments inflicted.
  • Prisoners will be treated humanely, as outlined later in this document, and cannot be compelled to pay fines nor forced to work in service of their work.
  • In suits at common law, where the value in controversy shall exceed 100,000 Spesos, the right of trial by jury shall be preserved.
  • In all capital crime prosecutions, the accused shall enjoy the right to a speedy and public trial by an arbitration except wherein the accused obstructs the pursuit of justice.

The New Frontier Sheriff's Department[edit]

The New Frontier Sheriff's Department (abbreviated from here on out as NFSD) is the premier law enforcement body active within the Frontier Sector. They are an organization that is commissioned, contracted and funded by NanoTrasen, to uphold Space Law throughout the sector. While they serve a similar role to the Security department onboard a NanoTrasen station, they are not direct employees of the corporation, having more in common with their fellow contractors in the sector than their removed cousins employed in the core sectors, and contractors are encouraged to treat their fellows with respect. They have a responsibility to ensure that the rights of their fellow contractors are upheld and that criminal activity is kept at a minimum.

You can read more about the NFSD's Standard Operating Procedures here.

The NFSD holds the capability to detain an individual under suspicion of criminal acts. A person that is reasonably suspected of having committed a crime may be detained. In detainment, the crewman is held on-site or moved to a safer location, may be restrained at officer's discretion and will remain as such for the time necessary for officers to determine the circumstances of the incident and whether to effect an arrest or not. A detainee may be either released or arrested.

The NFSD holds the capability to arrest an individual for criminal acts. An arrest will occur when a detainee is being charged with criminal acts. The charges have to be pressed by any officer of the NFSD, or by a member of the crew. Additionally, the Sheriff, or lacking one, the Station Representative, may issue an arrest warrant with probable cause, taking full responsibility for the warrant being lawful. When arrested, the perpetrator is taken to a secure vessel for interrogation and processing.

The NFSD holds a Class 2 Contraband License and may be authorized to use lethal force to the extent necessary to render opponents combat-ineffective while at a severe tactical disadvantage or under threat to the sector as a whole. The NFSD holds the authority to police the space within the New Frontier sector and are authorized to engage and subdue any threats against the station, crews, the sector, or NanoTrasen at large.The NFSD holds the capability to issue and carry out sentencing as stated in Space Law. All sentencing must keep in mind a contractor's basic rights and follow all suitable regulations. Officers take full responsibility for their sentencing and carrying out thereof.

Members of the NFSD, if given a felony level charge or higher, or are found to be acting in a manner unbecoming of an officer of the law, are compelled to be removed from service and demoted back to a civilian position.

Security Guards[edit]

Security guards are not part of the NFSD, but instead a member of Frontier Staff. Their responsibility is primarily to ensure that peace is kept on the primary stations of the sector, to aid the Station Traffic Controller in enforcing traffic violations, and to keep the Station Representative safe.

For most intents and purposes, the Security Guard is considered to be a member of the NFSD at the rank of Deputy, and is therefore afforded all the rights and responsibilities of the NFSD as outlined in this document. There are three key differences

  • They are supervised by the Station Representative, not by the Sheriff.
  • They are expected to defer to the NFSD in matters of sentencing and enforcement, should the NFSD become involved.
  • The Security Guard is expected to perform their duties primarily stationside. They do not nor should they declare No Fly Zones, as outlined later on in Space Law, nor be pursuing criminals across the entire sector beyond their jurisdiction onboard sector stations.

Sheriff's Authority[edit]

The Sheriff of the New Frontier Sector is the premier authority and final word for the interpretation of Space Law across the entire sector, and answers directly to Central Command. The Sheriff is the leader of the New Frontier Sheriffs Department, and is accountable for hiring, firing, demoting or promoting members of the NFSD as well as handling their pay.

Bounties[edit]

The Sheriff, and in their absence any Station Representative, are able to post criminal bounties which may be pursued by any bounty hunters or vigilantes within the sector. Bounties must be posted with the crime they had committed, who the affected parties are, and a reasonable bounty fund which is reasonable to pay by station funding. Fines collected may be used to pay for the bounty, but assets seized may not.

Pardons[edit]

The Sheriff, and in their absence any Station Representative, may pardon employees accused or sentenced for crimes if deemed in the best interest of the sector. Only misdemeanor and felony crimes may be pardoned. Central Command must be contacted to receive permission to pardon a capital crime. The Sheriff may be held legally responsible for criminal actions committed by those who they pardon. Pardons must be announced via the communications console whenever they are performed.

Captain's Authority[edit]

The Captain of a NanoTrasen vessel, in the absence of the NFSD, is the premier authority and final word for the interpretation of Space Law aboard their vessel, although they are held accountable for their actions by their crew and by the NFSD. Afforded to them are both the right to execute members of the crew, within just circumstances, as well as the ability to hire, fire, demote, or promote crew members at will

Execution Privileges[edit]

The Captain of a NanoTrasen vessel may perform executions without the need for a trial under certain circumstances. This means they have the authority to personally execute any member of their crew unless they're already in the custody of the NFSD. These circumstances would be threats to:

  • Authority: Attempting or threatening to undermine the Captain's command, with the capabilities to do so, via illegal means.
  • Command: Attempting or threatening to cause bodily harm to the Captain or a member of command while in the Captain's presence, and has the capability to do so.
  • Vessel: Attempting or threatening to cause physical damage to the vessel while in the Captain's presence, and has the capability to do so.

When an execution is carried out, the NFSD must be notified of the death in written form, detailing the reason for execution, the time of execution, and all witnesses present for the execution. This must be done in a timely manner, else such death will be declared as murder.

Mutiny[edit]

If the Captain is found, by a member or members of their crew, to have committed a crime of Class B Misdemeanor or worse, then they may start a lawful mutiny. A mutiny is defined as a coordinated effort to lawfully depose the Captain of a NanoTrasen vessel for committing unlawful acts. In the event of a mutiny, loyalists are tasked with subduing the mutineers with non-lethal force, and should not resort to lethal measures even if ordered to by the Captain, except in acts of self-preservation. The NFSD must be called to take custody of the Captain.

  • If the mutiny is unsuccessful, only the crewmembers who led the mutiny may receive an execution sentencing. Other mutineers may be demoted and/or imprisoned, as appropriate.
  • If the mutiny is successful, the crewmembers who led the mutiny may select the new Captain. The new Captain is responsible for ensuring those taken prisoner are brought to NFSD custody.

When a mutiny begins, the crewmembers starting the mutiny must send a message to admins to notify the admin staff of what is about to occur, although prior authorization is not required. If the mutiny is called into action without a good roleplay reason, participants may risk being banned.

Prosecution And Legal Definitions[edit]

Synthetics[edit]

All synthetic entities, including, but not limited to, robots, cyborgs and drones, issued by NanoTrasen are considered property of NanoTrasen, regardless of their origin or cognitive status. Synthetics created and fabricated on a vessel operated by any of NanoTrasen active contractors will be considered property of the contractor that created them and they will keep all rights, ownership and responsibility of said synthetic. Synthetics dispatched to Frontier Station from outside of the sector may be recruited by any contractor willing to care for them, at which point they become property of the contractor as stated above. Damage or destruction of a synthetic will be treated as damage of personal and company property and will be persecuted accordingly. The owner of the synthetic unit must maintain, repair and regulate the systems of the synthetics they currently own and they will be liable for any and all damages an improperly maintained unit causes to a person or property. In the case of a damaged unit being lost, it must be returned to its lawful owner. If no owner is found, then the unit must be sent to the Sheriff or Station Representative and be reprogrammed or decommissioned.

A functional cyborg that is removed from the crew for any reason, or when the crew is disbanded, is free to seek a new crew as it desires.

Companions[edit]

All service animals, familiars, companions, and pets, issued by NanoTrasen, created by NanoTrasen employees, or on board a NanoTrasen vessel, are considered property of NanoTrasen, regardless of their origin or cognitive status. To harm or kill any such companions is treated as damage of company property and will be persecuted accordingly.

Contraband[edit]

Contraband are substances, devices, weapons, chemicals, or other objects that are restricted or forbidden from people to possess or make use of without the appropriate licensure. It is important to note that Space Law defines contraband, not the notifications from the heads up display. Just because an item is unlabelled, or that it cannot be processed by standardized contraband disposal methods does not mean it is not contraband. Any contraband that cannot be processed should be destroyed.

The lists of examples are not exclusive. They include, but are not limited to the examples given. The Sheriff, and in the absence of one the Station Representative, has the final say on what items are contraband and which category they belong.

There are three categories of contraband, as follows:

Class 1 contraband contains all lethal, less than lethal and non-lethal weapons not otherwise covered by higher classifications. Class 1 contraband includes but is not limited to gunpowder weapons, laser weapons, protokinetic weapons, magic, swords, stun batons, and so on. All contractors retain a right to purchase, acquire and use class 1 contraband for lawful purposes. Any item used to commit a felony or capital crime becomes labelled as class 1 contraband, including but not limited to hardsuits, jetpacks, tools, utensils, and so on. If class 1 contraband is used for a felony or capital offence, the impugned contraband is to be permanently seized. Contractors who commit multiple felony or capital violations, or who resist arrest violently, may lose the right to use class 1 contraband.

Class 2 Contraband contains: all explosive devices not otherwise covered by higher classifications; toxins or pyrotechnic chemicals held by a non-authorized chemist, objects which can be used to non-destructively or non-invasively gain unauthorized access to NanoTrasen secured areas; exclusively restricted and/or authorized use weapons or equipment, uniforms and EMPs; NFSD or Command vessels, fully automatic weapons, and weapons with unregistered modifications (unless those modified weapons are also C3). Class 2 contraband may only be used by command and security personnel, or personnel with written authorization from the Sheriff, or, in their absence, the Station Representative. Possession of class 2 contraband is otherwise an offence governed by the misdemeanor or felony possession laws.

Class 3 contains the following items. They are not limited to the provided examples:

  • All explosives of the following:
    • Covert (mines, hot potatoes, explosive pens);
    • Military grade (incendiary or explosive grenades, C4 charges);
    • Highly destructive (nuclear bombs, syndicate bombs).
  • Hostile corporate or non-corporate clothing, symbols, or technology (Non-NanoTrasen issued scarves, jumpsuits, hats, headbands, pens, cigarettes, figurines, uplinks, telecrystals, agent IDs, encryption keys, weapons, radio jammers, implants, etc.)
  • Syndicate vessels (Hunter, Infiltrator)
  • Restricted or classified equipment possessed by an unauthorized individual (Emergency Response Team hardsuits, pulse weapons).
  • High caliber or excessively large weapons (Pirate Cannons, X-Ray Cannons, Laser Cannons, grenade launchers, Temperature Guns, Miniguns, etc.).
  • Concealed weaponry (cane sabers, energy daggers)

No person or vessel is to have class 3 contraband at any time. Note that high caliber weapons which may be found stocked in a security armory or are acquired from the NFSD uplink catalogue are considered class 2 contraband. Possession of class 3 contraband is an offence governed by the misdemeanor or felony possession laws.

For the purposes of removing a holoparasite or holoclown, NFSD is authorized to kill those implanted with those devices by temporary execution, immediately thereafter taking all steps to resuscitate them and provide appropriate medical treatment.

Contraband Chemicals[edit]

The creation, retention, and distribution of certain chemicals, particularly those of a harmful, disruptive, or mind-affecting nature, require permits as well, written by either the Sheriff, Director of Care, or Station Representative. In order to possess or imbibe these chemicals, the distributor must have a permit labelling them as a licensed chemist or as a class 2 contraband holder, and the buyer must receive paperwork confirming that they received the chemicals or substances, and in what quantity or condition.

Excessive or irresponsible distribution can result in the revocation of licenses at the discretion of Command staff. NFSD officers are not to imbibe any mind-altering substances.

It is generally safe to assume that a chemical is legal if it does not fall into one of the categories listed below. However, the Sheriff, or in their absence the Station Representative, has the final say on which chemicals fall under which categories. The categories are not limited to the stated examples. Any chemical that fits into the categories below, that the owner does not have the appropriate permit for, is considered to be C3 and disposed of accordingly.

The following chemicals and substances are considered C1 contraband if the owner has a signed receipt from a licensed chemist:

  • Mind-altering chemicals (space mirage, laughter)
  • Mind-altering substances (cannabis, rainbow cannabis, etc.)
  • C2 contraband chemicals that are prescribed in the treatment of specified medical conditions (narcolepsy medications, etc.)

The following chemicals and substances are considered C2 contraband, or requires the owner to be a licensed chemist:

  • Toxins (toxin, bungotoxin, amatoxin)
  • Acids (ferrochromatic acid, polytrinic acid)
  • Debilitating chemicals (pax, chloral hydrate, synaptizine)
  • Pyrotechnics (phlogiston, napalm, fluorosurfactant, smoke and EMP reactions)
  • Combat stimulants (ephedrine, desoxyephedrine)

The following chemicals and substances are considered C3 contraband. Possession is illegal at all times:

  • Hostile corporate or non-corporate synthetic chemicals
    • Examples include romerol, vestine, and chemicals made from vestine such as mute toxin, nocturine, hyperzine, lexorin, and tazinide. This is not a complete list.
  • Chlorine trifluoride
  • Diseases, viruses, and forms of biological warfare.

Chameleon Contraband[edit]

There exists a type of item that exists with capabilities to disguise itself as another item. Voice masks and agent IDs are the most noteworthy of these items, but plenty of other items, clothes, and weapons exist that either change their form or hide their true capabilities via appearing or disguising as another innocuous item. These 'chameleon' items are considered to be C3 contraband, even if they do not appear to be so on the heads up display or if they are not accepted by the contraband transfer machines, and are to be confiscated as any other piece of contraband would be.

Warrants, Stops, and Searches[edit]

Searches[edit]

A personnel search is a seizure of the objects in a person's vessel, backpack, hands, coat, belt and pockets. If any contraband is found during a search the officer may choose to further the search into a detainment or simply confiscate the restricted items. After the search is conducted all legal items are to be returned to the person. The right of the employees to be secure in their persons, vessels, papers, and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be violated, and no warrants shall issue but upon proper cause, supported by oath or affirmation, and particularly describing the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized. Potential grievances and suspicions of unlawful proceedings and maltreatment are to be directed up the Chain of Command and resolved judicially, enlisting legal counsel, if available.

Warrants[edit]

A warrant is a signed document stating security may search a specific person or place, the offense that is suspected of having been committed, and the reason why such a search is warranted. A warrant regarding a personnel requested by an officer may be approved by the Station Representative, Sheriff or the Captain of the person being searched. A Captain may give permission for security personnel to search their ship, but must sign a warrant if they wish to authorize security personnel to search their crewmembers. An authorized person may only sign a warrant if they are satisfied, on the information put before them, that there is a reasonable basis that the execution of the warrant would assist in the investigation of a crime. The authorized person signing the warrant takes full responsibility that the warrant is lawful, and may be subject to various penalties if the power is abused. The process of physically writing a warrant may be disregarded if the appropriate command member personally accompanies the officers throughout their search.

Exigent Circumstances[edit]

Law enforcement may, upon reasonable suspicion or belief that a crime is actively occurring aboard a vessel or by a person, may board that vessel or search that person to investigate the crime without a warrant. Valid circumstances for exigent circumstances to apply may include:

  • A reasonable belief that contraband or illegal weaponry for which the subject holds no permit, or an item used in the recent commission of a crime is being held or transported.,
  • Reasonable belief that the vessel must be boarded to prevent bodily harm or death, and immediate action must be taken.,
  • Reasonable belief that a crime is being performed aboard the vessel, and that immediate action must be taken to stop it in the public interest.,
  • An individual under active pursuit by law enforcement flees into, or through the vessel.

Vessel Operation And Traffic Guidelines[edit]

Vessels operating near, to, and from Frontier Station, or are within NanoTrasen space are beholden to the requirements below, regardless of origin.

Bluespace Anomalies And No Fly Zones[edit]

Occasionally the turbulence of bluespace will deposit wrecks, asteroids, or hostile vessels into the sector. If the NFSD deems the object a threat to the sector at large, a high value target, or the contents of which are intended to be used for illegal activity, they may enforce a no-fly-zone up to a maximum of 300 meters for the safety of employees operating in the region. Vessels that violate the No Fly Zone may be charged with trespassing or breaking and entering. Any officer may declare a No Fly Zone over Common radio, generally by the highest ranking officer available. Sheriffs will often announce NFZs over an announcement console, but this is not strictly required to post and enforce an NFZ. Casual violations of an NFZ should be asked to leave before enforcing. Flagrant, intentional, or aggressive NFZ violations are to be handled at the officers' discretion.

The Safe Zone[edit]

Frontier Outpost, and the area 200 meters around it, have been designated a safe zone. Crimes and violations occurring within the safe zone have increased penalties and often result in direct intervention by Central Command. Artifacts, anomalies, aggressive wildlife, and sentient creatures with harmfully aggressive instincts are not permitted on Frontier Outpost, and vessels bearing such are not permitted within 200 meters of Frontier Outpost. Vessels are not to conduct private business within 200 meters of the safe zone, nor are they to abandon excessive quantities of gear, supplies, or research on Frontier Outpost. This is to ensure that there is a sufficient amount of fair competition between different vessels.

Declared Salvage[edit]

Any vessel that has no active crew and is unresponsive on radio signals, may be claimed as legitimate salvage after the salvage procedure is followed:

  • First, attempt to contact the captain or crew of the vessel. If they are not present on the vessel, attempt to use the radio.
  • If the captain or crew is unresponsive, announce the name of the vessel you are attempting to salvage and begin a timer of twenty minutes.
  • During that time, continue making regular attempts to reach the vessel's owner. A good rule of thumb is once every five minutes or so.
  • After multiple attempts are made and a period of 20 minutes waiting since the first attempted call, the vessel becomes legal salvage and is considered the property of the salvager.

Legal salvage that is docked to stations should be undocked and moved 200 meters away from them before salvage begins. If there are any unconscious crew on the vessel to be salvaged, it is the responsibility of the salvager to ensure that they are safely relocated to a cryo chamber. Anything on their person is NOT legal salvage and must be respected, though it is permitted to temporarily use their ID card to sell a legally salvaged vessel before returning it immediately after.

Traffic Guidelines[edit]

The Station Traffic Controller (from here on referred to as the STC) is responsible for ensuring that vehicles maintain safe and legal operations. By default, they have jurisdiction around Frontier Outpost and any and all trade stations across the sector. They are permitted, by request or with approval of the Station Representative via written permit, to be given jurisdiction over any station in NanoTrasen space. Ships visiting any station which is under the jurisdiction of a STC is subject to the laws as outlined below. The STC holds the capability to direct the flow of traffic, to deny docking to any vessels as long as they have just cause to do so, to issue traffic citations and the fines associated with them, to refer reoffenders or those who refuse to pay citations to the NFSD for prosecution, to undock and tow ships, and to declare abandoned vessels legal salvage. Enforcement of traffic law and the issuing of fines is done at the discretion of the STC on duty.

Declaration Of Identity[edit]

Ships within 200 meters of a station under jurisdiction must have their IFFs enabled and visible. Those who attempt to dock with disabled IFFs or refuse to enable IFFs upon being reminded may be found guilty of a traffic violation.

Docking Timers[edit]

Upon docking to the station, ships have 10 minutes to conduct any business they may have. Ships under jurisdiction that violate this rule may be found guilty of a traffic violation and forcibly undocked from the station. Violations that exceed 20 minutes in length without response from the owner may result in the vessel being declared legal salvage, if the vessel is found to be abandoned.

The Station Representative, Sheriff, and STC are able to issue docking extensions that increase the time limit, or docking exceptions that permit for an extended stay on one of the docks, with or without condition, often to provide services or conduct business. This extension may be given verbally or via permit. Any party authorized to provide extensions or exceptions may revoke them at any time.

Docking exceptions may not be issued to ships docked directly to Frontier Station, though they may be extended with the permission of the Station Representative.

NFSD vessels and Station Staff vessels are exempt from docking time requirements.

Safe Conduct[edit]

Speeds near stations under jurisdiction should be kept slow, so as to minimize the chance of harming contractors operating EVA or ships that may be under repair. Ships are expected to be reasonable in their docking, and not ram nor damage the station. Ships are also expected to not block more docks than are necessary, nor prevent other ships from departing, to the best of their ability. Violators may be charged with traffic violations and/or reckless operation as appropriate.

Docking Procedure[edit]

When approaching or docking to a station under the jurisdiction of an STC, the procedure is as follows. Failure to follow these procedures may result in traffic violations being issued. When speaking to the STC, include your IFF signature (the string of letters and numbers associated with your vessel, such as LV-678, EV-000, QT-559, or so on) so the STC can identify the ship en route.

Approach[edit]

  • Hold position 100 meters or further away from the station, and contact the STC for instructions and permission to dock. You may provide your intentions once aboard the station for docking context.
  • Follow the instruction of the STC to the best of your ability, moving towards the dock that they indicate for your vessel.
  • Slowly proceed to the docks, being careful not to strike any other ships or EVA crews in the process, and acquire a good docking contact that blocks as few docks as possible.

Departure[edit]

  • Declare departure to the STC before leaving the station to alert them of your departure. Not doing so may be considered a traffic violation.
  • Attempt to keep space within 200m of the station clear. Move carefully to ensure you do not hit EVA crews or other vessels.

Emergencies And Docking Procedures[edit]

Oftentimes, either due to a medical emergency, an imminent risk to life and limb, or in pursuit of a criminal, emergency responders such as medical crews or NFSD shuttles may not have time to directly follow guidelines. Vessels docking in such a manner are encouraged to provide at least a rudimentary explanation as to why they are breaking docking procedure, and if they have a sufficient reason to do so, it is recommended that the STC encourages the safety of life and limb over strict adherence to the guidelines. NFSD vessels, outside of these emergency situations, are expected to abide by traffic guidelines as much as any other vessel, and are encouraged to, in turn, be respectful to the STC doing their job.

Traffic Citations[edit]

Traffic citations may be issued for violations of traffic law and docking procedure. Individuals issued a citation are responsible for going to the Station Representative, Sheriff, or STC to argue your case and/or pay their fine. Those who refuse to do so may be found guilty of traffic noncompliance and brigged. Fines collected are to be deposited in either the Frontier Station or NFSD station budgets. They are not to be kept for personal use by the individual who collects the fine.

Maritime Legal Status[edit]

Peaceful Vessels[edit]

Peaceful vessels are defined as those, regardless of origin, that are unarmed and operating in space for the intents and purposes of conducting lawful business. A peaceful vessel should not be attacked without warning by the NFSD. It can be fired on only if it repeatedly fails to stop when ordered to do so or resists being boarded by the NFSD fulfilling a legal warrant or in pursuit of a wanted criminal, at which case it becomes a belligerent vessel. The safety of non-hostile merchant vessels is to be guaranteed by all parties. When boarding a peaceful vessel, all parties must attempt negotiation before hostile action is undertaken. A peaceful vessel that surrenders must be treated with dignity and respect at all times.

Belligerent Vessels[edit]

Belligerent vessels are vessels that refuse to stop for the NFSD when ordered to do so, are being used by a fleeing criminal to escape justice, that escape the NFSD in a chase, or that commit flagrant violations of No Fly Zones (such as ignoring hails, ramming NFSD ships or the target of the NFZ, and so on, when NFSD are already present at the scene. This does NOT apply to vessels that are already conducting operations in the ABSENCE of NFSD presence). When chasing a vehicle, the NFSD is authorized to halt a vehicle using the means available at their disposal, and are not required to further hail the vehicle. Adequate steps must always be taken to ensure the safety of the crew. In cases where a vessel's crew is harmed in an act of boarding, efforts must be made to rescue and resuscitate them. This would usually mean taking the crew on board and transporting them to a medical facility, or allowing the intervention of medical personnel once the conflict is over. Belligerent vessels may be impounded by the NFSD if their owners abandon ship to escape justice. Any damages sustained to a vessel the course of a legally permitted boarding action is the responsibility of the criminal to fix. After the individual being pursued has been captured and has served their sentence, the vessel is considered peaceful once more.

Hostile Vessels[edit]

A hostile vessel is a vessel that is openly operating under the banner of an enemy of NanoTrasen and is conducting hostile operations in the sector. Examples of these include such as a Wizard Federation Towers, Cult Bloodmoon Stations, Syndicate Battlecruisers, or facilities embedded in large asteroids whose passengers attack nearby contractors on sight. Often labelled as Unidentified Vessels (though not all hostile vessels are unidentified vessels), hostile vessels are NOT protected by space law and are considered threats to the sector that are encouraged to be contained. It should be noted that contraband and pirate shuttles are NEVER hostile vessels; they are to be considered peaceful or belligerent vessels instead, as appropriate to the situation.

Foreign Vessels[edit]

The sector is encouraged to allow non-hostile visitors permission to dock upon outposts throughout the sector or otherwise engage in peaceful business during normal operating conditions. They may be searched upon arrival and escorted at the discretion of the NFSD. Visitors may request asylum, which is at a Station Representative's, or in their absence a Sheriff's, discretion to decide, and they will be given as much or as little access throughout the sector as they deem fit. Visitors are afforded the basic rights permitted by Space Law while present in the sector. All visitors aboard a NanoTrasen vessel are subject to Space Law, and should a visitor break the law and flee aboard their own vessel, the NFSD is authorized to board it in pursuit. NanoTransen contractors who are visiting a foreign vessel are obligated to follow both the laws of the vessel and Space Law, to their best ability. If an individual commits a crime aboard a NanoTrasen station and then flees to an unaffiliated party's vessel, the Captain of that vessel is authorized to grant or deny asylum. Foreign vessels' sovereignty must be observed and any decisions of their command to deny or restrict entry are to be respected.

Trial[edit]

In criminal prosecution, a trial cannot be requested when only misdemeanor charges are being pressed. The defendant may request a trial when felony charges are being pressed but are called to order at the discretion and ability of the potential Judge. If the defendant is accused of a capital crime, they are entitled to a trial. Legal counsel is permitted, and in fact a right of the accused if it can be obtained, but it is not required to stand trial. In all cases, defendants are permitted to waive their own right to trial.

The format of the trial is to be an arbitration court hearing, presided by a Judge that is typically a Station Representative or Sheriff. Should no Frontier Command Staff be available (or are involved with the case in a way that renders them biased), but a trial needs to be held, it is permissible to grant a temporary promotion to the position of Head of Courtroom, with the access and privileges equivalent to Sheriff, who shall preside over the trial and shall be granted this commission for the time and purpose of holding the trial.

In this hearing, the Judge directly examines the defendant and a representative of the prosecution best familiar with the case to present their case on why the defendant should be cleared or convicted of their charges. While there is no strict requirement on the duration of a trial, all parties involved, including the judge, prosecution, and defendant, have the right to a speedy trial, and if so invoked, the trial should be less than 20 minutes if at all possible, with each side given 6 minutes to argue their case.

Either party may appeal for the selected judge or prosecutor to have a conflict of interest, resulting in a new judge and/or prosecutor to be selected. This may only be applied when the Judge can be proven beyond a reasonable doubt to obtain strictly personal gain from either verdict or when the Prosecutor may be proven such gain from a Not Guilty verdict. Sheriffs are not automatically considered to be biased against the criminal simply because they are NFSD.

In the event that the defendant is held in contempt of court, the necessity of a trial may be waived and judgement may be passed by the Judge without further delay. A convicted criminal is to be sentenced by the NFSD as appropriate. If the Judge is a Head of Courtroom and not a Sheriff or Station Representative, this means that they usually are not the one passing sentence.

Trials may also be held for civil disputes, requested by any crewman against any other, preferably presided by the Sheriff or Station Representative. In cases where the civil dispute is in regards to a total value of 100,000 spesos or greater, either party has the right to request a trial by jury.

Legal Ethics[edit]

A lawyer has a paramount duty to the court. Any statement made by a lawyer cannot knowingly be false, or induce the court into error. If a lawyer becomes aware that a statement made earlier is no longer true, or was never true, then they must inform the court as soon as it is practicable to do so.

A lawyer has a duty to their client. They must act on the instructions of their client, zealously advocate for their client and they must maintain the confidentiality of anything their client has said, provided that the client hasn't expressly waived that confidentiality. Evidence that is a result of a breach of confidentiality cannot be used in court to convict a person or charge them with an offence. The duty of confidentiality persists even after the lawyer no longer represents the client. If there is a conflict between a lawyer's duty to the court and to their client, then the lawyer must withdraw from the matter and stop representing that client.

Do note, however, that this does not mean that a lawyer is not allowed to represent a client who did the charges in question. If a client tells a lawyer that they did the crime, they cannot lie to the court and outright say that they are innocent. However, the lawyer CAN argue that there isn't enough evidence to prove that there is probable cause, or perhaps they can argue that there are mitigating circumstances that reduces or eliminates the client's probability. If the client demands that the lawyer lies to the court outright by, for instance, insisting that the lawyer informs the court they didn't do something they admitted to doing, however, the lawyer should withdraw their representation, but note that withdrawing representation does mean you are still subject to your duty of confidentiality.

Pressing Charges[edit]

A person may be charged with a crime if and when there is found to have been probable cause to have committed an act in the nature and fashion described by a particular criminal charge. Probable cause is defined by the expectation that a reasonable person, when presented with the situation and evidence, comes to the conclusion that it is more likely than not to have occurred.

Multiple counts of one crime can be charged. For illegal actions concerning the sector at large, each incident separated by a reasonable period of inaction, lawful conduct, or another crime is interpreted as one count. For crimes directly bereaving a person (violent acts, theft of personal property etc.), the same applies, in addition to each victim being one count.

Charges that are a direct escalation of one another in nature cannot be simultaneously held against the perpetrator if they pertain to the same incident and the same victim/item, if applicable. Then, only the most severe of these charges apply. For instance, one cannot be found guilty of vandalism, sabotage, and grand sabotage in the same singular offense. In another example, one cannot be found guilty of manslaughter and murder in the same singular offense.

Sentencing[edit]

In sentencing a crime, it is recommended to defer to the Bailiff or Sheriff, however, any NFSD member is authorized to pass a sentence. Station Representatives and Security Guards are also permitted to pass a sentence, though they should defer to the NFSD when doing so if possible. The precise sentence is determined by the person passing the sentence and is at their discretion, though may be overruled by those higher in the chain of command. They are encouraged to use common sense in weighing the punishment against the severity of each charge separately as it is being applied, taking into account the broader circumstances of the crime and the modifiers further below.

  • First, list all committed crimes and how many counts of each.
  • Select one of the charges in the highest category. This is the primary offense. You may choose any punishment between the minimum and maximum for this offense.
  • Each other crime is a secondary offense. You add the minimum punishment for these offenses.
  • Consider if community service is a suitable punishment. If so, offer it to the convict in place of or in exchange for a reduced sentence now.
  • If choosing to offer a fine, and the crimes committed are of at most a Third Degree Felony, add up the total fine and offer it to the convict to pay. If the convict agrees to pay the fine, the collected money MUST go to the Frontier Outpost or NFSD station budget. If the primary crime is a Second Degree Felony or higher, or the convict refuses or is unable to pay, move to the next step.
  • Add up the brig time. If the total brig time is 60 minutes or higher, you have the option to replace the sentence with Extended Confinement, though it is not required to do so. In the case that it is chosen to do so, it is also recommended that parole be considered after the initial time is served.
  • Administer the brig time as necessary.

Sentence Modifiers[edit]

When applying charges, certain circumstances can apply, altering the severity of the punishment. In the event such circumstances would result in release or Extended Confinement, the sentencing should be deferred to the ranking member of the NFSD if at all possible.

Principals and Accessories to a Crime[edit]

All of the following persons may be charged for the commission of a crime. The degree of involvement is a sentencing consideration.

  • Principal: Has committed a crime.
  • Solicitation: Encouraging, bribing, requesting, or instructing someone to commit a crime, with the intent that the person being solicited commit the crime.
  • Accessory: Assisting in the commission of a crime, without participating in the crime itself, knowing that a crime will be, has been, or is being committed.
  • Attempts: Attempting to commit a crime, with the intent to carry out that crime.
  • Conspiracy: Planning to commit a crime, with the intent to eventually carry out that crime

Self Defense[edit]

A person is not guilty of an offence if they reasonably believed that the offense was necessary for the following:

  • To protect their life, or the life of others.
  • To emancipate themselves, or another person, from unlawful imprisonment.
  • To protect their property, or that of another person.
  • To prevent criminal trespass onto their vessel.

And -

  • The amount of force used is proportional to the threat faced, or was the only means available.

A person is not entitled to rely on self-defense of property when the criminal charge is murder. If an accused raises self-defense, the prosecution must disprove the elements of self-defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to convict the accused. If a person honestly believes their acts are necessary for self-defense but the belief is not reasonable in the circumstances as the accused knew them at the time, then it acts as a mitigating factor in sentencing or lowers a murder conviction to a manslaughter conviction.

Necessity[edit]

A person is not guilty of an offence if they reasonably believed the offense was necessary in order to avert a greater and imminent peril. This peril must have affected the accused or someone in their charge, they must have honestly believed that their offending was necessary in order to avoid that peril and the offending must not be out of proportion to the peril avoided.

Duress & Entrapment[edit]

A person is not guilty of an offence if they were coerced into the commission of the offence, fearing an immediate apprehension of death or serious injury from the person coercing them. Nor are they guilty if the accused was coerced by a member of Law Enforcement into committing the crime, where the individual would have not otherwise done so.

Aggravating Factors[edit]

These factors may be used to increase brig time, depending on the actions of the offender.

  • Recidivism: To be arrested, charged and convicted for criminal behavior multiple times within one shift. If someone is a recidivist, then for each instance of reoffense, increase the weight of the offense by one- the first time you evade arrest, it is a Class C Misdemeanor, the second time you evade arrest, it becomes a Third Degree Felony, the third, a Second Degree Felony, and so on.
  • Aggravation: To commit a crime with reckless abandon, with lethal intent, clear signs of lack of regret or remorse, or with particularly severe implications for the community. Several crimes have aggravation clauses that dictate how they are changed and why for aggravated cases- for cases that do not have such clauses, increase the weight of the offense by one.

In both cases, the maximum increase for these crimes is a First Degree Felony. A non-capital crime cannot become a capital crime because of aggravation or recidivism.

Extenuating Factors[edit]

These factors may be used to reduce brig time, depending on the actions of the offender.

  • Cooperation: Sharing actionable intelligence with the authorities, turning oneself in, confessing to crime. An early guilty plea entitles an offender to a mandatory 25% discount to their sentence.
  • Good Behavior: Peaceful compliance with the NFSD, clear signs of remorse concerning committed crime, peaceful conduct in brig. This may result in an overall reduction in time or an early release.

Punishments[edit]

The various ways in which someone who is found guilty of a crime may be sentenced.

Normal Punishments[edit]

  • Brigging: The convicted is stripped of their equipment and placed in the communal brig for a period of time. Prisoners have rights and responsibilities as outlined further below.
  • Fining: The convicted is charged an amount of Spesos, or the monetary equivalent thereof, by the department, to be deposited in the NFSD or Frontier Outpost station budget for departmental use.
  • Community Service: The convicted is to work, without pay, by completing assigned tasks at the requirement of Frontier Command Staff. This can be assigned instead of another punishment, or in accompaniment of it. The convicted can never be forced to work; if they are unable or unwilling to perform the community service, they will be brigged instead.
  • Demotion: The convicted's staff contract is downgraded or revoked, revoking access accordingly. This is almost always an additional punishment, and is issued if the offender commits a felony while station staff or NFSD.
  • Catch & Release: The convicted is taken into NFSD custody, processed, and searched with contraband confiscated, before being immediately released afterwards. This is not the same as dropping or clearing charges.
  • Extended Confinement: The convicted is brigged for the remainder of the shift. This may be with or without parole, with or without work-release agreements, or more.

Capital Punishments[edit]

  • Execution: The convicted has their life terminated. Whenever an execution is performed, regardless of trial or not, a communications console announcement must be made detailing who was executed and why..
  • Exfiltration: The convicted is extracted via a dispatched prisoner transport to Central Command for processing, interrogation, and further, more formalized prosecution- a process that inevitably will occupy them for the remainder of the shift..
  • Decorporealization: Stripping of the convict's mind off their own body into a more restrictive, less capable form.

Fines[edit]

While there is no upper ceiling on fines accumulated, they must be paid in full and cannot be mixed with brigging; the convicted is either fined or brigged, but not both. The convicted cannot be forced to pay a fine; if they are unable to or unwilling, they will be brigged instead. Note that crimes that are a Second Degree Felony or above cannot be fined for and carry a mandatory brigging sentence.

Seizure of Assets[edit]

Assets and Spesos gained as part of criminal activity may be confiscated. Examples of this include goods stolen from their owners, contraband sold unlawfully, or funds gained from smuggling activity. Property and funds are to be returned to its owner if possible, as well as used to make whole the victim of a crime, or added to the NFSD or Frontier Outpost budget otherwise. Assets seized in this way as a result of a crime are not counted towards any resulting fine. Officers are not to retain any seized assets for personal use.

NFSD is permitted to make reasonable assumptions regarding these assets, such as assuming that a large sum of money in the possession of a criminal arrested for black marketeering recently after committing the crime was obtained illegally. Even if it is argued that only part of the money was earned illegitimately, it all may be seized. NFSD are not permitted to increase fines or punishment for money that could not be secured before it entered the convict's bank account, nor is it permitted for NFSD to force someone to withdraw any amount of money from their personal accounts. Only what is physically present at the moment of arrest may be seized.

Community Service[edit]

At the behest of the Bailiff or Sheriff, a prisoner may be granted community service as a replacement for or reduction of brig time, or as a condition of parole from extended confinement. This community service will be unpaid, overseen by a parole officer, and may only be granted for non-violent offenders. Prisoners cannot be forced to undertake community service.

Treatment Of Prisoners[edit]

Prisoners still have certain rights that must be upheld by law enforcement:

  • Prisoners must be granted adequate medical care. They should also be proffered counseling, if available.
  • Prisoners must be provided access to the common radio channel so long as it is not abused. They are not permitted to have departmental radio.
  • Prisoners must be granted clothing, food, water, shelter and safety to the best of ability of the NFSD. If the brig is no longer safe, confinement must be established in another location.
  • The belongings and vehicles of prisoners that are not confiscated are to be protected and returned to them upon the conclusion of their sentence. Prisoners in Extended Confinement should be permitted to sell their vessels and gear using the terminals provided at the outpost, or their value as provided is to be reimbursed as outlined in Space Law.
  • Prisoners are permitted legal counsel, if available.
  • Prisoners should be granted freedom of movement and kept in the communal brig unless there is an undue risk to life and limb. Prisoners may be sent to solitary confinement if they are unruly.
  • Prisoners who repeatedly attempt to escape, who excessively damage their cell or the brig to the point where it significantly degrades or destroys the public functions of the brig, who render the brig spaced or uninhabitable, or who inflict serious bodily harm to officers or their fellow inmates, may be executed without trial.
  • Prisoners may request or may otherwise be given parole or bail without prompt by the Bailiff or Sheriff.
  • Prisoners are allowed visitors at the visitation window. Visitors may enter the brig only at the discretion of the Bailiff or Sheriff. If so permitted, they must be searched, have a valid reason of entry, and must be supervised and escorted at all times.
  • Prisoners sentenced to execution via a trial may request the method of execution. These options are limited to spacing, electrocution, firing squad, or lethal injection.
  • Prisoners are always permitted to sell their belongings and enter cryogenic storage. They should be advised that upon doing so, they'll be incarcerated at Central Command and be unable to return for that shift. If they are spotted in the sector again that shift, Central Command should be informed.

Officers who come across criminally insane individuals who act unsightly or unreasonably during their detainment or incarceration should reach out to Central Command for advice.

Crimes And Punishments[edit]

Crimes are categorized by severity. Each severity has different penalties for sentencing.

Any crime of Second Degree Felony or higher cannot be issued a fine, and they must serve brig time or some other punishment.

Certain crimes are labelled that compensation may apply. If that is the case, then, in addition to the sentence, the criminal may be financially responsible for the damage caused. This, unlike other fines, is assigned in addition to other sentencing, and assets may be seized to pay off this fine.

Maximum - Capital Punishment

Remember, as per server rules, contractors are not permitted to commit capital crimes.

First Degree Felonies[edit]

Minimum - 15 minutes

Maximum - 25 minutes

Offense Definition Aggravation Notes
Manslaughter To effect violence upon a person, resulting in their death.
  • A death of a person that doesn’t qualify as murder and isn’t fully accidental will typically fall here.

Unauthorized Decorporealization To unlawfully strip a being’s mind from their own body. May be punished by decorporealization in turn, despite it being a capital punishment.
  • The most common case of this is turning someone into a cyborg without their consent.
  • Whether the act was authorized and in what way is up to Command. When a part of a criminal sentence, it is always authorized.

Third Degree Felonies[edit]

Minimum - 5 minutes OR $20,000 fine.

Maximum - 10 minutes OR $40,000 fine.

Offense Definition Aggravation Notes
Battery To effect unsolicited or unlawful physical contact with a person or to thusly administer a substance upon them, directly or indirectly. If the victim is placed into critical condition, it becomes a first degree felony. In this case, it is referred to as First Degree Battery.
  • Battery can have a great variety of causes with a great variety of severity. Keep that in mind when sentencing.
  • Battery requires physical contact to be made and an attack to have happened. If only a threat was made, then the crime is assault instead.
Felony Possession To unlawfully possess class 2 or class 3 contraband, which has been used to commit or perpetuate a felony or capital offense.
  • This offense always accompanies other serious offending.
  • Simply possessing the contraband, and not using it in another crime, is misdemeanor possession instead.
Black Marketeering To sell or distribute restricted items or restricted/illicit substances.
  • As with possession, authorizations are determined by Frontier Command.
  • Distribution of drugs by an unlicensed chemist falls under this category.
  • Being found with a neatly-folded note from a smuggling drop is probable cause for black marketeering.
  • When seizing assets involved in the crime, do your best to discern what was obtained legally and what was obtained illegally for confiscation. You are permitted your best judgement in cases of stacking items, such as Spesos.
  • Ensure seized assets are either disposed of or added to the NFSD or Frontier Station station account as appropriate. They are not to be pocketed for personal use.
Rioting To engage in a public disturbance which involves damage to property.
  • Similar to battery, the severity of this crime is highly varied.
Vehicular Assault To recklessly and intentionally use a vehicle as a weapon against an occupied vessel, or person.
  • It must be reasonably shown that the action was deliberate with intent to cause harm or damage.

Hooliganism To intentionally engage in disruptive conduct. If you are engaging in hooliganism, you may be brigged indefinitely until you begin behaving yourself, to the best judgement of the arresting officer.
  • Includes but is by no means limited to harassment, slander, radio spam, and other bad behavior that is annoying and disruptive to other contractors in the sector.
  • If they ask you to stop doing something and you don’t, you can likely be charged with this.
  • This is a catch all for malicious disruption that isn’t otherwise chargeable.

Traffic Citations[edit]

Unlike other crimes, traffic citations have unique punishments, usually fines. These fines can be issued alongside or in addition to other punishments such as forced undocking or the declaring of your vessel legal salvage. Traffic Citations, notably Reckless Operation, can come with criminal charges as well that can be prosecuted by the NFSD at their discretion. If a contractor does not pay their fines in a reasonable amount of time, the case can be referred to the NFSD under Traffic Noncompliance.
